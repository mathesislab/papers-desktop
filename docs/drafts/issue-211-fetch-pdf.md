Papers Desktop has no numbers of its own: it fetches one PDF when asked and offers *Open in browser* when the download fails. So I made a small probe. 41 DOIs: 32 from my library (3 each from the 13 most common DOI prefixes: Copernicus, Nature, AGU, Elsevier, Springer, Science, IGS, Wiley, AMS, PNAS, IOP, Cambridge, Royal Society) and 9 from 2024 in the same journals. For each: the OpenAlex work, a download of `best_oa_location.pdf_url`, the page of `https://doi.org/<DOI>` and its `citation_pdf_url`, with the user agent of the app (`Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; Papers Desktop/0.7.0)`), from an institute network.

**What came out**

- 30 of the 41 are open for OpenAlex (gold 9, bronze 12, hybrid 4, green 5). A program got a PDF for 12 of them, all published versions: Copernicus, Cambridge (IGS), IOP, Nature Communications, AMS.
- Some publishers refuse a program whatever the open access: Wiley (AGU, RMetS), PNAS, Science and the Royal Society answer 403 with a Cloudflare challenge (`Just a moment...`), for the page and the PDF. AMS answers 202 with a challenge for some articles. Elsevier's doi.org link ends on a `linkinghub` page that redirects with a meta refresh, then sciencedirect answers 403. Springer answered 200 with an HTML page for the PDF of a hybrid article. So the `%PDF` check is needed: a 200 is not enough.
- `best_oa_location` often has no `pdf_url` (7 of the 30), while the publisher's `citation_pdf_url` works (Copernicus GMD and CP, Nature Communications). The publisher's page is then the only way to the published PDF. The repository locations of green works mostly have a landing page and no `pdf_url` (4 of 5).
- For the 18 open works without a published PDF, the accepted or submitted locations gave 2 PDFs (5 refused or HTML, 11 without `pdf_url`). One of the two, `submittedVersion` at archimer.ifremer.fr (10.1016/j.quascirev.2024.108635), is the supplementary information of the article, not the article. So `version` is a hint: right for the published locations, less so for the repositories.
- An AMS article of 2010 that OpenAlex calls closed gave its PDF from `citation_pdf_url` (AMS opens its journals after a year, or the institute's subscription: I cannot tell which). The publisher's page is worth a try for a closed work too: a refusal gives no file anyway.
- Supplements: none of the 41 pages has a `citation_supplementary_material` tag. Links with `supp` in them are on the pages of Copernicus, Nature, Cambridge and IOP, so the names of the links are the only signal, publisher by publisher (open question 2).

**For the proposal**

1. When the published version is refused and an accepted or submitted version is attached instead, the message could still give the link to the published one, to download in the browser.
2. Preprints and accepted manuscripts will be common for whatever is not gold (Wiley, Elsevier, PNAS, Science). Such an entry could get a tag (`preprint`, `accepted`), so that `papers list --tag preprint` finds them, and there could be a way to replace that PDF when the published one can be had, e.g. `papers file fetch --upgrade` (the entries whose PDF is not the published version), or `--again` replacing that file instead of adding a second one. Today it is `papers file rm` then `papers file attach`.
3. `--missing` on a large library (open question 3): with these refusals, a run would mostly hit challenges at Wiley, PNAS and Science. Asking before more than 50 seems right, and a summary at the end (attached, which version, refused with their links) would help.

For Papers Desktop: once `papers fetch --pdf` exists, the app could use it instead of its own OpenAlex lookup, if the output says the version, the source and, for a refusal, the link to open (with `--porcelain`, or a distinct exit status).
