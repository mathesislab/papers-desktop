Your papers, from first sight to citation

Papers Desktop follows the journals, preprints and blogs of your field, searches all of science, keeps the papers you choose in your .bib files with their PDFs, opens them in tabs, and finds and cites any of them in a keystroke. Plain files, no account, no database.

Follow

The new papers of your field, the best first

Subscribe to journals, preprint servers, news sites and blogs. Each new paper gets a score from your keywords and from how close it is to the papers already in your library, so the ones you would read come first, and the rest are a glance away.

Go through the list with the keys: j k to move, s to shortlist, m to mark as read, x to hide, a to add to the library. A blog post leads to the paper it is about; one for later can be snoozed.

Find

Search all of science

The Search tab asks OpenAlex, an open index of hundreds of millions of works, with the same words and filters as your library: a:, y:2015-, j:. The papers you already have are marked, and the closest ones of your library shown beside each result.

Follow a paper's citations and references, add it in one key, or keep a search: Subscribe turns it into a feed of the papers to come. Online ▾ sends the same query to Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, arXiv, PubMed and others.

One loop, not four apps

A search you like becomes a feed. A paper in a feed is one key from your library, with its PDF. And your library tells the feeds what to rank first: the more you keep, the better they get.

Keep

Your library, in plain files

Your .bib files are the library: from papers, JabRef, BibDesk or a Zotero export. The library window shows them in a table that the same search filters, with views (recently added, no PDF, broken file links, possible duplicates) and your tags.

Add a paper by its DOI or arXiv id, with its free PDF; edit, tag and attach files; ⌘Z undoes any change. Export the papers of a tag to a .bib file: the references of a manuscript.

Read

Read in tabs

Space opens a paper in a tab, its details beside it. On macOS the PDF shows as in Preview, smooth to scroll and zoom, with find and the outline. The papers you had open come back the next time.

Everything has a key: / to search, ⌘1 ⌘2 ⌘3 for the library, the feeds and the search, ⌃Tab from tab to tab, ⌘W to close.

Cite

Any paper, a keystroke away

Press ⌥Space in any app and type a few letters of an author, a title or a year: rob finds Robinson, y:2010-2015 keeps those years, a pasted reference finds its entry.

↵ opens the PDF, ⌘. copies [@key] for Markdown, ⌘L \cite{key} for LaTeX, ⌘B the BibTeX, ⌘⌥C the citation in your style. ⌘K lists every action.

Always up to date

Change a .bib file with any tool, or let a sync client do it: the app reloads it at once.

Quick

A library of thousands of entries loads in a few tens of milliseconds, and the results follow your typing.

Citation styles

Copernicus, AGU, AMS, Nature, Science, APA and more are built in, and any CSL style works.

Share and drag out

Copy the title as a link, the DOI or the citation, formatted where it pastes. Drag a paper out to drop its PDF in a mail, a chat or a folder.

From any app

Select a reference anywhere and choose Services → Search Papers to find it in your library. On Linux, press Ctrl+Alt+Shift+Space.

Out of the way

It lives in the menu bar and hides when you go back to work. The library window is in the Dock only while it is open.

A command line too

papers-rs, the same program that writes your library, installs from the app menu. It works with the papers command line.

Open source

Free software under the MIT license, written in Rust with Tauri. It updates itself.

Download

macOS

For Macs with Apple silicon.

Download for Mac
First start

Papers Desktop is not notarized by Apple yet, so macOS blocks it the first time. Drag it to Applications and open it; then open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. Or, in Terminal:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Papers Desktop.app"

Then choose your .bib files: menu-bar icon → Settings…

Linux

A .deb for Ubuntu 22.04 or later, and Debian.

Download for Linux

AppImage, for other x86-64 distributions

First start

Install the .deb with

sudo apt install ./Papers-Desktop-linux-amd64.deb

and run papers-desktop, or make the AppImage executable (chmod +x) and run it. The hotkey is Ctrl+Alt+Space, and Ctrl+Alt+Shift+Space searches the selected text. Under Wayland (Ubuntu's default) apps cannot take a global hotkey: add a custom shortcut in Settings → Keyboard that runs papers-desktop, which shows the panel, and one that runs papers-desktop --selection.

The Linux version is new: it is built and tested automatically, but has not yet been tried much on real machines. Problems and feedback are welcome as issues.

Once installed, the app updates itself (Settings → Updates). All versions are on the releases page. To build it yourself, see the README.
