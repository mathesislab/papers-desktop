Your BibTeX library, a keystroke away

Papers Desktop is a search panel for your .bib files and the PDFs they link to. Press ⌥Space, type a few letters of an author, a title or a year, and open the paper, or copy its citation or its BibTeX. The library window shows it all, and opens the papers to read in tabs. Your library stays plain files: no database, nothing to import.

Search the way you remember

Every word must match, from the start of a word: rob finds Robinson. Filters narrow it down: f: for the first author, y:2010-2015 for years, j: for the journal, has:pdf for the papers with a PDF, quotes for a phrase, - to leave something out.

Paste a reference from a paper or a web page to find the entries closest to it: the entry of its DOI, else those with most of its authors, title and year.

Results come newest first, with the matched words in bold. A strip shows the matches of each year: drag across it to keep a range of years.

Cite in one key

Open the PDF with ↵, copy [@key] for Markdown, \cite{key} for LaTeX, the BibTeX entry, or the citation, formatted, with ⌘⌥C. ⌘K lists every action.

The preview shows the first page, the citation in your style (Copernicus by default) and the entry, as formatted or as written in the file.

Your library in a window

Open Library from the menu-bar icon, or ⌘O in the panel: a table of your papers that the same search filters, with views in the sidebar (recently opened, no PDF, broken file links, possible duplicates) and your tags. Sort, move, resize and choose the columns.

Export the selection, or the papers of a tag, to a .bib file: the references of a manuscript.

Read in tabs

↵ opens a paper in a tab of the sidebar, its details beside it. On macOS the PDF shows as in Preview, smooth to scroll and zoom, with find and the outline. The papers you had open come back the next time.

Everything has a key: / to search, ⌘1 for the library, ⌃Tab from tab to tab, ⌘W to close.

Plain files

Reads .bib files as they are, from papers, JabRef, BibDesk or a Zotero export. The PDFs stay in your folders, where any app opens them.

Always up to date

Change a .bib file with any tool, or let a sync client do it: the panel reloads it at once.

Several libraries

Choose one or more .bib files; the panel searches them all.

Quick

A library of thousands of entries loads in a few tens of milliseconds, and the results follow your typing.

Citation styles

Copernicus, AGU, AMS, Nature, Science, APA and more are built in, and any CSL style works.

Share

Copy a snippet with the title as a link, the link, the DOI, or the citation as text or HTML. They paste formatted in documents and mails, as plain text elsewhere.

Drag out

Drag a paper from the list to drop its PDF in a mail, a chat or a folder, and the citation from the preview to drop it in a document.

From any app

Select a reference anywhere and choose Services → Search Papers to find it in your library. On Linux, press Ctrl+Alt+Shift+Space.

Recent papers

The empty panel lists the papers you opened or copied last, with a reminder of the keys and filters.

Out of the way

It lives in the menu bar, opens with its hotkey and hides when you go back to work. The library window is in the Dock only while it is open.

Made with papers

A companion of the papers command line, which adds PDFs to the library with the right names. Papers Desktop needs nothing else to run.

Open source

Free software under the MIT license, written in Rust with Tauri.

macOS

For Macs with Apple silicon.

Download for Mac
First start

Papers Desktop is not notarized by Apple yet, so macOS blocks it the first time. Drag it to Applications and open it; then open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. Or, in Terminal:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Papers Desktop.app"

Then choose your .bib files: menu-bar icon → Settings…

Linux

A .deb for Ubuntu 22.04 or later, and Debian.

Download for Linux

AppImage, for other x86-64 distributions

First start

Install the .deb with

sudo apt install ./Papers-Desktop-linux-amd64.deb

and run papers-desktop, or make the AppImage executable (chmod +x) and run it. The hotkey is Ctrl+Alt+Space, and Ctrl+Alt+Shift+Space searches the selected text. Under Wayland (Ubuntu's default) apps cannot take a global hotkey: add a custom shortcut in Settings → Keyboard that runs papers-desktop, which shows the panel, and one that runs papers-desktop --selection.

The Linux version is new: it is built and tested automatically, but has not yet been tried much on real machines. Problems and feedback are welcome as issues.

Once installed, the app updates itself (Settings → Updates). All versions are on the releases page. To build it yourself, see the README.
